# KATOUWA — two-minute English pitch (spoken script)

**0:00–0:22 · Hook**

Imagine living above the water, in a community whose livelihood and history are tied to the mangroves. In Santa Rosa de Agua, Venezuela, environmental degradation is not an abstract chart. It is part of everyday life.

**0:22–0:45 · The problem**

Satellite data reveals environmental signals over time. Yet one pixel cannot tell us what a fisher has observed, which species is missing, or what a place means to its people. We need to keep both perspectives visible without confusing them.

**0:45–1:20 · Live demo**

Meet **KATOUWA**: Two Skies, One Territory. First, we select a location and a NASA data variable. We retrieve a time series, inspect quality flags, and show its annual trajectory. The tool calculates an exploratory Theil–Sen slope and Kendall trend test. It asks four questions: **what changed, where, how much, and is the trend statistically significant?** We show uncertainties as prominently as results.

**1:20–1:44 · Local knowledge**

The second sky is the community. A 2007 study documented local experiences of mangrove degradation. With informed permission, future records can be placed alongside satellite signals. These are independent sources of evidence—not numbers to be mechanically merged.

**1:44–2:00 · Impact**

We also propose La Ñeruna: a biomimetic floating-island concept for future testing. KATOUWA cannot restore a lake in one weekend. It can make environmental change more transparent, more testable, and more accountable to the people living with it. **The satellite observes from above. The territory knows from within.**

**Important presenter note:** If MODIS/POWER retrieval was not successful on the demo machine, say “we have implemented the NASA retrieval workflow but could not fetch live observations here.” Do not imply unverified trends are real.
